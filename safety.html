🛡️ Online Safety

Stay Safe, Protect Your Information, and Avoid Online Scams

The internet provides many opportunities for learning, communication, and entertainment. However, it also comes with risks that can affect users' privacy and security. This section provides practical information and safety tips to help individuals recognize online threats and protect themselves while using digital platforms.

Article 1: How to Recognize Online Scams

Online scams are dishonest activities that attempt to trick people into sharing personal information, sending money, or giving access to their accounts.

Common Signs of Online Scams

  • Messages claiming that you have won an unexpected prize.
  • Requests for passwords or verification codes.
  • Suspicious links asking you to log in.
  • Offers that seem too good to be true.
  • Messages that create urgency or pressure you to act immediately.

How to Protect Yourself

  • Do not immediately trust unexpected messages.
  • Check the sender's identity.
  • Avoid clicking suspicious links.
  • Never provide passwords or verification codes to strangers.
  • Verify offers through official websites or communication channels.
  • Report suspicious messages to the appropriate platform.

Article 2: How to Create Strong Passwords

Passwords help protect personal accounts and prevent unauthorized access. Using weak or repeated passwords can make accounts more vulnerable.

Tips for Creating Strong Passwords

  • Use a combination of uppercase and lowercase letters, numbers, and symbols.
  • Choose passwords that are difficult to guess.
  • Avoid using birthdays, names, or other easily discovered personal information.
  • Use different passwords for important accounts.
  • Consider using a trusted password manager.
  • Enable two-factor authentication when available.
Remember: Never share your passwords with other people.

Article 3: Protecting Personal Information Online

Personal information includes details such as your full name, home address, phone number, passwords, and financial information. Sharing these details carelessly can put your privacy and security at risk.

Ways to Protect Your Information

  • Avoid posting sensitive personal details publicly.
  • Check the privacy settings of your accounts.
  • Use secure and trusted websites.
  • Avoid accessing important accounts through suspicious links.
  • Be careful when connecting to public Wi-Fi.
  • Do not share verification codes with anyone.

Article 4: Understanding Two-Factor Authentication

Two-factor authentication, also known as 2FA, is an additional security feature that requires users to verify their identity through another method after entering their password.

Why Is It Important?

  • Adds another layer of account protection.
  • Makes unauthorized access more difficult.
  • Helps protect important personal accounts.
  • Provides additional security even if a password becomes known to someone else.
Helpful Tip: Enable two-factor authentication on important accounts whenever the service provides it.

Article 5: Staying Safe on Public Wi-Fi

Public Wi-Fi networks in cafes, airports, and hotels are convenient, but they are often unsecured. Hackers can sometimes intercept data sent over open networks, putting your personal accounts at risk.

Risks of Public Wi-Fi

  • Unencrypted networks allow others nearby to view the websites you visit.
  • Fake Wi-Fi hotspots (often named after the venue) can trick users into connecting to a hacker's device.
  • Automatic connection settings may join unsafe networks without you noticing.

How to Stay Safe

  • Avoid accessing online banking, shopping, or sensitive accounts while on public Wi-Fi.
  • Confirm the exact network name with staff before connecting.
  • Turn off auto-connect features and file sharing on your device.
  • Use your mobile data hotspot instead when handling important information.
Helpful Tip: If you must use public Wi-Fi regularly, consider using a trusted Virtual Private Network (VPN) to encrypt your connection.

Article 6: Managing App Permissions and Privacy Settings

Many apps and websites ask for access to your location, camera, microphone, or contacts. Reviewing these permissions helps ensure you only share what is truly necessary for the app to work.

Common Permissions to Check

  • Location: Does a simple calculator or wallpaper app really need your exact GPS location?
  • Camera and Microphone: Limit access to apps used for photos, videos, or calls.
  • Contacts and Photos: Avoid granting full access unless you are actively sharing or syncing.

Steps to Tighten Your Privacy

  • Choose "While Using the App" instead of "Always" for location access.
  • Review your social media privacy settings so only friends or trusted contacts can see your posts.
  • Periodically audit your device's privacy dashboard and revoke unnecessary permissions.
