TechBridge Community Resource Directory

Free tech help and safe e-waste drop-offs, close to home in Taguig and Metro Manila. New to computers or smartphones? Not sure where to get online, learn something new, or throw away an old phone? You're in the right place—nobody here expects you to already know things. Everyone starts somewhere.

How to Use This Page

This directory is a simple list of free or low-cost places and programs near you. Items marked with Verify are placeholders—confirm the address, schedule, and services with the place itself (or your barangay hall) before publishing or visiting.

1. Pick what you needGetting online, learning skills, or recycling old gadgets.

Good to Know

  • It's okay to be a beginner. Library and community staff help first-time users every day.
  • Free doesn't always mean unlimited. Some places have time limits on computers or ask you to register first.
  • Services change. Schedules, programs, and drop-off rules are updated from time to time, so always confirm before you go.
  • Bring a friend or family member. Going with someone makes your first visit easier.

💻 Category 1 Extra Guide

Getting Online for Free: Tips, Law & Common Questions

Quick Tips for Getting Online for Free

  • Ask first. Libraries, barangay halls, and community centers can tell you if they have Wi-Fi or computers.
  • Check the Wi-Fi name and password. Staff will usually tell you. Don't join a network that has no clear owner.
  • Know your limits. Some places allow 30 to 60 minutes per person during busy hours Verify.
  • Printing may cost a small fee. Bring coins or small bills, just in case.
  • Go early. Mornings are usually quieter and easier for first-timers.
Did You Know? The Philippines has a law that supports free internet access in public places (the Free Internet Access in Public Places Act). Many government offices, public parks, and public hubs offer free Wi-Fi—ask your barangay or city hall what's available near you Verify.

Common Questions (Computer & Wi-Fi Access)

Do I have to pay to use library computers?Many are free for registered users, but some have small printing fees. Ask the staff.
Can I use my own laptop or phone?Usually yes. Ask where the Wi-Fi and charging spots are.
What if I don't have a library card?Most places can register you on the spot with a valid ID.

What You'll Learn in a Beginner Class

  • How to turn on, connect, and charge your phone or computer
  • How to send a message, make a video call, and write an email
  • How to search the internet and tell trustworthy websites from fake ones
  • How to create a strong password and keep it private
  • How to spot common scams, like fake links and messages asking for your OTP
  • How to fill out an online form and save or print a copy

Tips to Get the Most Out of a Class

  • Ask questions. If you're confused, someone else probably is too.
  • Take notes in your own words. Write the steps down so you can repeat them at home.
  • Practice little and often. Ten minutes a day helps more than one long session.
  • Use your own device when you can. Practicing on your own phone makes it easier to remember.
  • Don't be shy about repeating. It's fine to attend a session more than once.
Need to Report a Scam? If you receive a suspicious message, link, or call, don't click or reply. You can report cybercrime to the Philippine National Police Anti-Cybercrime Group or the Cybercrime Investigation and Coordinating Center (CICC) Verify current hotline numbers and websites before publishing.
♻️ Category 3 Extra Guide

What Counts as E-Waste, Pre-Recycle Checklist & Alternatives

Old phones, chargers, and batteries should never go in regular trash. Metals and chemicals like lead and mercury can harm people and the environment, and batteries can leak or catch fire.

Item Where to take it Before you go
Old mobile phonesMobile network store bins, community drivesBack up, factory reset, remove SIM and memory card
Chargers, cables, earphonesMobile network store bins, barangay MRFBundle together in a bag
Used batteries (AA, AAA, button cells)Battery drop-off points, collection drivesKeep separate, tape the ends of larger ones
Laptops and desktop computersAccredited recyclers, collection drivesBack up and wipe or remove the hard drive
Printers, monitors, keyboardsAccredited recyclers, collection drivesRemove ink or toner if you can; call ahead
Small appliances (fans, rice cookers)Collection drives, accredited recyclersConfirm they are accepted first
Power banks and swollen batteriesAsk an accredited recycler or the city environment officeHandle carefully, keep away from heat, and don't charge them

Before You Recycle: A Simple Checklist

  • Back up your photos, contacts, and files
  • Sign out of your accounts (Google, Apple, Facebook, banking apps)
  • Turn off any "Find My Device" setting that locks the phone to you
  • Do a factory reset
  • Remove your SIM card and memory card
  • Gather chargers, cables, and batteries separately
  • Check the drop-off point's hours and accepted items

Other Options Before Recycling

  • Donate it. If it still works, ask a school, church, or community group—wipe your data first.
  • Repair it. A new battery or screen can give a gadget years of life.
  • Sell or trade it. Meet in safe public places and wipe your data.
  • Keep it safe until you can recycle it. Store old batteries in a cool, dry place away from children.

Common Questions (E-Waste)

Can I put my old phone in the regular trash?No. Phones and batteries should go to a proper drop-off point.
Is it free to drop off e-waste?Many community and store drop-offs are free, while some recyclers charge for large or bulky items. Ask before you go Verify.
Will my personal data be safe?Not unless you erase it yourself. Always back up and reset before dropping off.
What if my device won't turn on?You can still recycle it. Just remove the SIM, memory card, and any loose batteries if you can do so safely.
🛡️ Safety, Packing & Support

Staying Safe & Prepared: Complete Visitor Guide

Before You Go

  • Call or check online first to confirm hours, services, and any fees.
  • Bring a valid ID—many places need it to register you.
  • Charge your phone and bring a charger or power bank.
  • Write down your questions so you don't forget them.

Staying Safe in Person

  • Go during the day when you can, and tell someone where you're going.
  • Travel with a friend if carrying heavy items or going somewhere new.
  • Keep valuables out of sight in malls, jeepneys, and train stations.
  • Use official channels; be careful of people asking money for free government help.
  • Trust your instincts—if something feels off, leave and ask staff or barangay officials.

At a Community Tech Spot

  • Never share passwords or one-time codes (OTPs), even with someone helpful.
  • Log out of every account when finished, especially on shared computers.
  • Avoid banking or shopping on public Wi-Fi or shared computers.
  • Don't save passwords on a public computer if it asks.
  • Keep belongings close and ask staff/volunteers beginner questions.

Staying Safe Online

  • Use strong, longer passwords (such as random words with numbers).
  • Be wary of links/messages that sound too good to be true or rush you.
  • Look for https on websites where you type personal info.
  • Keep your phone updated and protect personal info under the Data Privacy Act.

At an E-Waste Drop-Off

  • Back up first, then do a factory reset and sign out of accounts.
  • Don't open or break batteries; keep swollen/damaged ones away from heat.
  • Put loose batteries in separate bags and tape metal ends if possible.
  • Use official drop-off points only.

Packing Checklist: What to Bring

  • Valid ID (and student or senior ID, if you have one)
  • Charged phone + charger/power bank, notebook & pen
  • Email address & mobile number written down, flash drive, coins for printing
  • Earphones; for e-waste, items bagged/sorted with data erased

Tips for Older Adults, Families, Students & Parents

  • Older adults & families: Take your time, ask for large text, keep passwords/OTPs private even from helpers, learn together, and talk about scams.
  • Students & parents: Check sign-ups and privacy settings, log out of shared computers, set screen-time habits, and back up school files in more than one place.

If Something Goes Wrong

  • Lost or stolen phone: Contact your mobile provider to block the SIM and change important passwords from another device.
  • Shared OTP or clicked suspicious link: Contact your bank or service right away and change passwords.
  • Unsure if a message is real: Don't reply; verify using an official website or number.
  • Damaged or swollen battery: Keep away from heat/flammables, don't charge or puncture, and ask a recycler or city environment office.

✅ Do

  • Call ahead to confirm hours and services
  • Back up and erase data before recycling
  • Log out of shared computers
  • Use official drop-off points
  • Ask staff when you're unsure

❌ Don't

  • Assume a place is open or offering a service
  • Hand over a phone with accounts still signed in
  • Save passwords on public devices
  • Throw batteries or electronics in the trash
  • Share your OTP or password with anyone
