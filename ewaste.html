♻️ E-Waste and Recycling

Promote Responsible Technology Disposal

Technology makes our daily activities easier and more convenient. However, discarded electronic devices can cause environmental problems when they are not properly handled or disposed of. This section provides information about electronic waste (e-waste), its possible effects on the environment, and responsible ways to manage unwanted electronic equipment (World Health Organization [WHO], 2024).

Article 1: What Is Electronic Waste?

Electronic waste, commonly known as e-waste, refers to discarded electrical and electronic equipment that is no longer being used, is damaged, or has become outdated (WHO, 2024).

Examples of E-Waste

  • Old computers and laptops.
  • Damaged or unused mobile phones.
  • Keyboards and computer mice.
  • Chargers, cables, and other electronic accessories.
  • Televisions and other electronic appliances.
Some electronic products contain materials that may harm the environment if they are not handled, recycled, or disposed of properly (WHO, 2024).

Article 2: Why Proper E-Waste Disposal Matters

Improper disposal of electronic waste can contribute to environmental pollution and other environmental concerns. Some electronic components contain hazardous substances that require proper handling and disposal (WHO, 2024).

Benefits of Proper E-Waste Management

  • Helps minimize environmental pollution.
  • Supports the recovery and reuse of valuable materials.
  • Encourages responsible consumption of electronic products.
  • Reduces the amount of unnecessary electronic waste.
  • Promotes environmental awareness and responsible disposal practices.

Article 3: How to Manage Electronic Waste Properly

Managing electronic waste properly helps reduce its negative effects on the environment. Individuals can contribute by following simple and responsible disposal practices (WHO, 2024).

Simple Ways to Manage E-Waste

  • Repair electronic devices whenever practical instead of immediately replacing them.
  • Donate working devices that are no longer needed.
  • Reuse electronic equipment whenever possible.
  • Bring unwanted electronics to authorized collection points or recycling facilities.
  • Follow local guidelines and proper procedures for electronic waste disposal.
Important Reminder: Avoid burning or dismantling electronic devices as a way of disposing of them. Always follow proper collection, handling, and recycling procedures (WHO, 2024).

Article 4: How to Reduce Electronic Waste

Reducing electronic waste starts with making responsible decisions when purchasing, using, and disposing of electronic devices. Taking proper care of technology can help extend its useful life and minimize unnecessary waste.

Helpful Practices

  • Purchase electronic devices only when they are needed.
  • Take proper care of devices to extend their useful life.
  • Repair damaged equipment whenever possible.
  • Reuse or donate working electronic devices instead of throwing them away.
  • Choose appropriate recycling options when devices can no longer be repaired or reused.
